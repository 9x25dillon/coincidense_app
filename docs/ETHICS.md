# Ethics

Three of this project's data domains carry obligations that are not satisfied by "the data
is public."

## Cave locations

The caving community withholds entrance coordinates deliberately. The reasons are concrete:
uncontrolled access damages irreplaceable formations, spreads white-nose syndrome between
bat colonies, and kills inexperienced visitors. Some locations are also protected by statute
on federal land.

This project publishes cave systems at the resolution its sources publish them, and no
finer. Where a source gives a system's general location but withholds the entrance, the app
does the same. No geocoding of narrative descriptions into precise points, no triangulation
from photographs or trip reports, no inference of withheld coordinates from survey data.

If a rendering choice would make it easier to locate an unpublished entrance, that is a
defect regardless of how much it improves the map.

## Tribal data sovereignty

Reservation boundaries are public government records. That does not make every use of them
appropriate, and it does not make this project's framing neutral.

Overlaying tribal lands with a missing-persons mystery layer is a rhetorical act. The
genre it borrows from has a long history of treating Native land as an atmospheric device —
a backdrop for the uncanny rather than the territory of living nations with governments,
jurisdiction, and their own accounts of their history. This project inherits that risk by
construction and does not get to be innocent of it.

Concrete commitments:

Nations are named as they name themselves. Boundary categories are kept legally distinct —
reservation, trust land, statistical area, ceded territory, ancestral territory are not
interchangeable, and flattening them is both a data error and a political one.

The MMIWG data is carried because it is a documented crisis with real reporting gaps and
real jurisdictional causes — the Oliphant jurisdictional gap, underfunded tribal law
enforcement, inconsistent database entry. It is present as *that*, sourced and explained.
It is not present as evidence for anything paranormal, and any rendering that lets it drift
in that direction has failed.

Where the historical explanation for a spatial pattern is known — and for reservation
locations it largely is, through removal and allotment policy — the app states it. Leaving
a documented history as an unexplained pattern is how mystification happens.

The CARE Principles for Indigenous Data Governance are the right frame for anything beyond
published boundary files. If this project ever moves toward community-level data, that
requires consent from the communities involved, not just an open license.

## Missing persons and victim dignity

These are real people and living families.

This app works with aggregates and published statistics. It does not host individual case
records, does not build searchable victim profiles, and does not render individual
last-known locations. NamUs case-level data exists and is public; that is not sufficient
reason for this project to republish it in a mystery-cluster context.

Names do not appear on the map. A family should not encounter a relative rendered as a data
point in an argument about caves.

Speculative causation is out of scope in the output surface. The app measures spatial
association and says so; it does not narrate what happened to anyone.

## The framing problem, stated plainly

A tool that overlays these three layers is one design decision away from being a machine
for manufacturing insinuation. The safeguards in
[`EVIDENCE_STANDARDS.md`](EVIDENCE_STANDARDS.md) and
[`METHODOLOGY.md`](METHODOLOGY.md) — mandatory confounds, visible null models, tier-locked
symbology, rendered absence — are not academic fussiness. They are the difference between
this project and the thing it would otherwise become.

If a future change makes the map more compelling by making the null model less visible,
that change is a regression, however good it looks.
